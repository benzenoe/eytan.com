Purpose & Context

Why These Changes, Why Now

The European launch model is not mass e-commerce. The website's job in Europe is to act as a catalogue and appointment engine: visitors browse the collection, qualify themselves, and book a private viewing. Paid campaigns on Meta, LinkedIn, and Google will drive Luxembourg, Paris, and Brussels traffic directly at this flow from Month 1.

Every specification below exists to protect that investment: if the booking flow, tracking, or compliance layer is missing when campaigns go live, ad budget converts to wasted clicks.

📄 Contractual Basis

This brief is the "Website advisory" deliverable defined in §3.1.1 of the Digital Marketing Services Agreement (executed 26 June 2026): a written brief to the Client's development team covering the changes required for the European market model, including booking mechanism specifications, European address formats, and tracking pixel implementation guidance.

Per §7, implementation is carried out by the Client's own development team. Per §3.2, website development is excluded from the Consultant's services.

Routing rule: budgets of €2,000+ become confirmed appointments; €500–2,000 enquiries are directed to the online shop for self-service purchase; under €500 receives the catalogue newsletter path.

Fast option: a Calendly embed (≈€15/month) connected to Kuntal's calendar delivers this without custom development and can be live in one day. A custom form matching the site design is the preferred end-state; Calendly is an acceptable Phase 1 implementation.

Why it's first: 100% of the ad budget points at this flow. It is the single conversion event the entire local strategy is built around.

2 European Section with a Dedicated European Price List

A dedicated European section (subdirectory or subdomain — dev team's choice) presenting the collection with:

  • European prices drawn from a separate pricing table — reflecting the landed Luxembourg cost of each piece (duties, shipping, VAT, and European margin) — not a currency conversion of the Indian price, which is how the site works today
  • European contact block: Luxembourg address, +352 phone, WhatsApp Business number
  • The "Book a Private Viewing" entry points (Spec 1) throughout
  • A legal/imprint page appropriate for EU consumer-facing commerce

⚠️ Key change from current behaviour: the site already auto-converts prices to EUR via geo-detection. Conversion is not the problem — the converted number is the wrong price for the European market. The product catalogue needs a second price field (or price table) per SKU for the European market, and the European section must read from it.

Why: a currency-converted Indian price ignores landed cost and positions the pieces incorrectly for the European market — every ad click lands on a price the business cannot honour.

4 GDPR Cookie Consent

A compliant consent management platform (CMP) on all European-section pages. Requirements:

  • Explicit opt-in before any tracking tag fires — Meta, LinkedIn, and GA4 tags must be blocked until consent is given
  • Google Consent Mode v2 integration (required for Google Ads measurement in the EU)
  • Reject option as prominent as Accept; preferences revisitable at any time
  • EU-facing privacy policy page covering data collected by the booking form and pixels

Suggested tools: Cookiebot, Usercentrics, or CookieYes — all integrate with GTM. Estimated effort: 2–3 days.

Why: we are the ones installing trackers and running EU ads — operating them without consent exposes the company to GDPR fines of up to 4% of global annual revenue. This is inseparable from Spec 3.
Tier 2
High Priority — Within 30 Days of Launch

First-Month Improvements

5 European Address & Phone Formats

All forms on the European section (booking, checkout, newsletter) must accept European conventions: postal-code-before-city address layouts, alphanumeric postcodes, and international phone formats (+352, +33, +32) with a country-code selector. Every data-capture point carries the GDPR consent checkbox from Spec 4.

Why: named explicitly in the contract (§3.1.1) — and a checkout that rejects a Luxembourg postcode kills the €500–2K self-service tier.

6 Extend Existing Geo-Detection to the Full European Experience

The site already geo-detects visitors and switches currency automatically. Extend that same mechanism so European visitors receive the complete European experience — the European section, the European price table (Spec 2), and the European contact details — not merely a converted currency figure. Keep a manual region switcher as override.

Why: the geo infrastructure exists; it currently switches the currency symbol but not the commercial experience behind it. Organic and referral traffic needs the same routing paid traffic gets.

7 Mobile-Optimized Booking Flow

Meta campaigns are predominantly mobile. The booking form must be a short, thumb-friendly, single-screen experience on mobile: large tap targets, native select controls, no horizontal scrolling, and a visible submit button without keyboard overlap.

Why: scoped down from a full checkout overhaul — in the concierge model the booking form is the checkout.
Tier 3
Recommended — Not Launch-Blocking

Performance & Depth

8 CDN for European Visitors

The site is served from India; European load times of 2.5–4s hurt both Google Ads Quality Score (raising CPC) and bounce rates. A Cloudflare free-tier CDN in front of the European section is a low-effort, high-return fix. Target: LCP under 2.5s from Luxembourg/Paris.

9 EU Payment Options for the Self-Service Tier

For the €500–2,000 self-service segment: EUR card processing that reliably accepts European cards, plus PayPal and Apple Pay / Google Pay. BNPL options (Klarna, iDEAL, Bancontact) are deferred — high-ticket sales close in person, not at an online checkout.

10 Language Selector — English, French & German

The European section requires all three regional languages — English, French, and German — with a user-facing language selector in the site header. Language must be the visitor's choice; geo-detection may set a sensible default, but never lock it.

  • Visible EN / FR / DE switcher in the European section header, persisted across pages
  • Native-quality (not machine) translations for product pages, the booking flow, and legal pages
  • Rollout order: English at launch → French → German, as translations are validated
Why: Luxembourg operates in all three languages, Paris requires French, and the German-speaking regional radius (Trier, Saarbrücken, Frankfurt) requires German. A trilingual selector is a trust marker for a Luxembourg-based luxury brand.

Descoped from the earlier Europe-wide audit

The original 9-market e-commerce audit included items that no longer apply to the local launch model:

  • Multi-currency (EUR/GBP/PLN/CZK) — EUR only in this phase
  • hreflang for 5 languages and per-locale sitemaps — one market, not nine
  • Trustpilot review-volume push — trust is built in person and via LinkedIn in the concierge model
  • Lab-grown diamond education hub — an SEO play for mass e-commerce, not appointment generation
  • Full checkout overhaul — self-service is now the secondary path

Launch-Readiness Checklist

  • GTM container installed; all four conversion events fire and verify in Meta Events Manager, LinkedIn Campaign Manager, and GA4 DebugView

  • EU visitors geo-route to the full European experience (extending the existing currency geo-detection)

  • Booking flow completes in under 60 seconds on a mobile device
  • ashth.eu and ashth.lu resolve to the European section (DNS by Eytan, after registrar access)
