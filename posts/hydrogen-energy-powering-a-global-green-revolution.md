From Grey to Green – The Spectrum of Hydrogen Types

Hydrogen comes in many colors (figuratively) depending on how it’s produced. Traditionally most hydrogen has been grey, made from natural gas without capturing emissions, but newer low-carbon types are rising.

Green Hydrogen

Produced by splitting water (electrolysis) using renewable electricity (solar, wind, etc.), yielding hydrogen and oxygen with zero CO₂ emissions. Green hydrogen is currently a small share of supply, but like solar and wind power, its cost is expected to fall sharply with scale.
https://www.iea.org/reports/global-hydrogen-review-2025

Blue Hydrogen

Made from fossil fuels (usually natural gas) via steam methane reforming, paired with carbon capture to trap the CO₂ byproduct. Blue hydrogen still generates CO₂ during production, but captures most of it for storage.
https://www.iea.org/news/low-emissions-hydrogen-projects-grow-as-policy-support-races-to-catch-up

White Hydrogen

Naturally occurring hydrogen found in underground geological formations. It requires no industrial production and could cost under $1/kg. Only pilot projects exist today.
https://www.iea.org/reports/global-hydrogen-review-2024/hydrogen-production

All hydrogen fuel is emission-free at point of use, producing only water vapor.

Safety Matters: Hydrogen’s Safety Profile

Hydrogen is non-toxic and disperses upward rapidly due to its low molecular weight. It is flammable and requires leak detection, ventilation, and proper materials to avoid embrittlement.

Industry has decades of experience handling hydrogen safely. Studies show hydrogen can be as safe as gasoline or natural gas when standards are followed.
https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-hydrogen

Driving the Future: Hydrogen in Transport

Fuel cell electric vehicles (FCEVs) emit only water. Models include Toyota Mirai and Hyundai Nexo. Over 90,000 FCEVs were on the road globally by 2024.

Asia-Pacific leads adoption. South Korea targets 6.2 million hydrogen vehicles by 2040.
https://www.iea.org/policies/6566-korea-hydrogen-economy-roadmap-2040

Hydrogen refuels in minutes and excels in heavy-duty transport. Airbus targets hydrogen-powered aviation by 2035.
