01 — The Three-Leg Thesis

Three bets.
One machine.

Each leg hedges the others. Together they form a structure where the structural return is the spread between Bitcoin's long-run appreciation and a low, regulated borrowing cost — compounding against a backdrop of dollar debasement.

₿

The Bitcoin is never sold.

This is not a fund that buys Bitcoin and sells it to buy property. The Bitcoin reserve is posted as collateral through regulated institutional channels to borrow euros at ~2–5% APR. The Bitcoin compounds untouched in regulated multisig custody. That spread — Bitcoin appreciation vs. borrowing cost — is the engine.

02 — The Leverage Engine

Borrow against Bitcoin.
Buy Portuguese property.
Repeat.

The Bitcoin reserve is not sold to fund acquisitions — it is posted as collateral through regulated channels to borrow euros, which acquire Portuguese real estate. The Bitcoin position compounds untouched.

The claim is not "free leverage."

The borrowing cost is real (~2–5% APR) and is included in every return illustration. The claim is that the spread between Bitcoin's long-run appreciation and a low, regulated borrowing cost is structural and persistent across a cycle. Conservative LTV is maintained at all times. Collateral health is monitored continuously with defined de-risking triggers.

09 — The Hard Questions

Five challenges.
Five honest answers.

Sophisticated investors will ask these questions. We have asked them of ourselves first. Below are the honest, complete answers — including the parts that are still open.

Challenge 01
"What happens to the Bitcoin when it drops 80%?"
We modelled this. See the interactive stress test above. At 40% origination LTV, the reserve can absorb a ~38% BTC drawdown before the first de-risking trigger activates — and that trigger sells real estate, not Bitcoin. A staged waterfall (65% → 75% → 85% LTV) means management is responding actively long before a margin call threatens the reserve. BTC has dropped 80%+ three times in its history. Each time, it recovered to new highs within 24 months. The risk is real. The mitigation is structural, staged, and transparent — and we are showing you the math, not asking you to trust us.
Addressed — stress-tested, waterfall defined
Challenge 02
"Why should I pay 20% carry on Bitcoin going up?"
You should not — and the structure is designed to reflect this. The 8% cumulative preferred hurdle must be cleared before the carry applies. The preferred liquidation preference (1×) protects investors on the downside. The carry compensates management for: sourcing and executing Portuguese real estate (illiquid, relationship-gated, bureaucratic); managing the BTC lending facility, LTV monitoring, and active de-risking; and operating the full Luxembourg SOPARFI + SPV legal structure across two jurisdictions. The BTC is the collateral engine. The work is in the real estate and the risk management. If you want pure BTC exposure, buy BTC. This instrument gives you BTC + leveraged EUR hard assets + institutional structure + preferred terms.
Addressed — carry is on the full structure, not the reserve alone
Challenge 03
"Name the lender. Who is actually doing the BTC-backed loans?"
The formation process involves shortlisting regulated lending counterparties who can provide BTC-collateralised facilities to a Luxembourg entity. Target profile: regulated custodian-lenders (category includes Sygnum Bank, institutional Bitcoin-backed credit desks at regulated European banks, and specialist crypto prime brokers with EU licences). We do not yet have a signed term sheet — this is an open item, and we are stating that clearly. The pilot SPV (Pebble Stone Farm) will be used to establish and test the lending relationship at a manageable scale before the full institutional raise. Any investor should treat the lending counterparty as an open due-diligence item until a name and terms are disclosed.
Partially open — lending counterparty is a formation-stage deliverable
Challenge 04
"Strip out BTC appreciation. What's the actual real estate return?"
Honest answer: the RE-only return is a respectable but unremarkable Portuguese property fund. ~40% of capital, leveraged ~2–3×, at 3–5% net yield, with 2–4% annual appreciation. That produces roughly 1.5–2× over 10 years before fees — a respectable euro return, but not why you're here. The structural alpha lives in the BTC reserve compounding while the RE deployment earns you current yield and hard-asset exposure. This is, at its core, a Bitcoin fund with a real estate income engine. We say that explicitly, because the LP base, risk profile, and regulatory classification follow from it. If you are allergic to BTC volatility, this is not the right instrument.
Addressed — the BTC thesis is primary; the RE is the yield and diversification layer
Challenge 05
"€100M pre-money with no assets — justify the valuation."
The €100M pre-money is the indicative conversion price for the convertible preferred — not an assertion of current enterprise value. The instrument is structured as a convertible precisely because the entity has no track record yet. The 1× liquidation preference means investors get their money back before any conversion value is realised. The preferred dividend accumulates at 8% PIK. The honest framing is: you are buying a protected entry into a structure at a pre-agreed conversion price, with downside protection via the liquidation preference, in exchange for providing formation-stage capital before the pilot SPV is closed. The €100M figure will be validated or renegotiated based on what the pilot demonstrates. Founding investors who join before the pilot closes are compensated for the risk via preferred terms, not a discounted price.
Addressed — instrument is a convertible preferred, not an equity valuation claim
