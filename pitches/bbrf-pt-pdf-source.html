The de-risking waterfall — three stages before BTC is ever threatened

Stage 1 (LTV >65%): Management reduces loan exposure by 15% via partial asset sale or additional equity contribution. No Bitcoin sold.
Stage 2 (LTV >75%): Reduce by 30%. One SPV liquidated. Loan partially repaid. Reserve intact.
Stage 3 (LTV >85%): Full margin call response. Loan repaid via RE sale proceeds. Bitcoin still not sold unless all other options exhausted.
The Bitcoin is a last resort, not a first response. Conservative origination LTV and active monitoring are the primary protection.

11 — The Hard Questions

Five challenges.
Five honest answers.

Sophisticated investors will ask these questions. We have asked them of ourselves first. Below are the honest, complete answers — including the parts that are still open.

Challenge 01
"What happens to the Bitcoin when it drops 80%?"
We modelled this — see the stress test on page 10. At 40% origination LTV, the reserve can absorb a ~38% BTC drawdown before the first de-risking trigger activates — and that trigger sells real estate, not Bitcoin. A staged waterfall (65% → 75% → 85% LTV) means management is responding actively long before a margin call threatens the reserve. BTC has dropped 80%+ three times in its history. Each time, it recovered to new highs within 24 months. The risk is real. The mitigation is structural, staged, and transparent — and we are showing you the math, not asking you to trust us.
Addressed — stress-tested, waterfall defined
Challenge 02
"Why should I pay 20% carry on Bitcoin going up?"
You should not — and the structure is designed to reflect this. The 8% cumulative preferred hurdle must be cleared before the carry applies. The preferred liquidation preference (1×) protects investors on the downside. The carry compensates management for: sourcing and executing Portuguese real estate (illiquid, relationship-gated, bureaucratic); managing the BTC lending facility, LTV monitoring, and active de-risking; and operating the full Luxembourg SOPARFI + SPV legal structure across two jurisdictions. The BTC is the collateral engine. The work is in the real estate and the risk management. If you want pure BTC exposure, buy BTC. This instrument gives you BTC + leveraged EUR hard assets + institutional structure + preferred terms.
Addressed — carry is on the full structure, not the reserve alone

Challenge 04
"Strip out BTC appreciation. What's the actual real estate return?"
Honest answer: the RE-only return is a respectable but unremarkable Portuguese property fund. ~40% of capital, leveraged ~2–3×, at 3–5% net yield, with 2–4% annual appreciation. That produces roughly 1.5–2× over 10 years before fees — a respectable euro return, but not why you're here. The structural alpha lives in the BTC reserve compounding while the RE deployment earns you current yield and hard-asset exposure. This is, at its core, a Bitcoin fund with a real estate income engine. If you are allergic to BTC volatility, this is not the right instrument.
Addressed — the BTC thesis is primary; the RE is the yield and diversification layer
Challenge 05
"€100M pre-money with no assets — justify the valuation."
The €100M pre-money is the indicative conversion price for the convertible preferred — not an assertion of current enterprise value. The 1× liquidation preference means investors get their money back before any conversion value is realised. The preferred dividend accumulates at 8% PIK. The honest framing is: you are buying a protected entry into a structure at a pre-agreed conversion price, with downside protection via the liquidation preference, in exchange for providing formation-stage capital before the pilot SPV is closed. The €100M figure will be validated or renegotiated based on what the pilot demonstrates.
