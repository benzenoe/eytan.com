Hydrogen Energy: Powering a Global Green Revolution

July 10, 2025 • Eytan Benzeno

Hydrogen Energy: Powering a Global Green Revolution

Figure: A fuel-cell vehicle (Toyota Mirai) refueling at a California hydrogen station, while an Air Products “SmartFuel” hydrogen delivery truck supplies the fuel. Hydrogen enables zero-emission transport with fast refueling similar to conventional fuels.

From Grey to Green – The Spectrum of Hydrogen Types

Hydrogen comes in many colors (figuratively) depending on how it’s produced. Traditionally, most hydrogen has been grey, made from natural gas without capturing emissions, but newer low-carbon types are rapidly gaining momentum.

Green Hydrogen

Green hydrogen is produced by splitting water via electrolysis using renewable electricity such as solar or wind power, yielding hydrogen and oxygen with zero direct CO₂ emissions. While it currently represents a small share of global hydrogen production due to higher costs, green hydrogen mirrors the early trajectory of solar and wind power — expensive at first, then dramatically cheaper as scale, learning curves, and manufacturing efficiencies improve.

IEA – Global Hydrogen Review 2025

Blue Hydrogen

Blue hydrogen is produced from fossil fuels, typically natural gas, via steam methane reforming combined with carbon capture and storage (CCS). While CO₂ is still generated during production, the majority is captured and stored underground, making blue hydrogen a low-carbon bridge solution that leverages existing gas infrastructure while significantly reducing emissions.

IEA – Low-emissions hydrogen projects

White Hydrogen

White hydrogen refers to naturally occurring hydrogen found in underground geological formations. It requires no industrial production and may cost under $1/kg if commercially extractable. Only pilot projects currently exist — notably in Mali — but if scalable, white hydrogen could be a game-changer due to its minimal environmental footprint.

IEA – Hydrogen production overview

All forms of hydrogen are emission-free at point of use, producing only water vapor when consumed in fuel cells or combustion processes.

Safety Matters: Hydrogen’s Safety Profile

Hydrogen is non-toxic, odorless, and disperses rapidly upward due to its extremely low molecular weight. Unlike gasoline vapors, it does not pool at ground level. However, hydrogen is highly flammable, requiring careful system design, leak detection, ventilation, and material selection to prevent embrittlement.

Decades of industrial use in refineries and chemical plants demonstrate that hydrogen can be handled as safely as conventional fuels when proper standards are followed. Modern storage tanks use carbon-fiber composites tested against impact, penetration, and fire exposure.

IEA – The Future of Hydrogen

Driving the Future: Hydrogen in Transport

Fuel Cell Electric Vehicles (FCEVs) generate electricity onboard using hydrogen, emitting only water. Commercial models include Toyota Mirai, Hyundai Nexo, and Honda Clarity. By 2024, more than 90,000 FCEVs were operating globally.

Asia-Pacific leads deployment. South Korea’s national strategy targets 6.2 million hydrogen vehicles by 2040, supported by over 1,200 refueling stations.

IEA – Korea Hydrogen Economy Roadmap 2040

Hydrogen’s fast refueling (3–5 minutes) and high energy density make it especially suited for heavy-duty transport, including buses, trucks, trains, maritime shipping, and aviation. Germany operates hydrogen trains on non-electrified lines, while Airbus targets hydrogen-powered commercial aircraft by 2035.

Fueling Industry: Hydrogen’s Role in Industrial Decarbonization

Hydrogen’s most transformative role may be in hard-to-abate industries.

Steel

Traditional steelmaking relies on coal and accounts for roughly 8% of global CO₂ emissions. Hydrogen can replace coal as the reducing agent. In 2021, Sweden’s HYBRIT project delivered the world’s first fossil-free steel to Volvo, with full commercial production planned by 2026.

HYBRIT – Fossil-free steel
Reuters – HYBRIT steel delivery

Chemicals and Fertilizers

Hydrogen is a core feedstock for ammonia and refining. Transitioning to green hydrogen enables low-carbon fertilizers and clean industrial feedstocks. Companies such as Yara and CF Industries are investing heavily in electrolyzer capacity.

The IEA identifies hydrogen as critical to decarbonizing these sectors.

IEA – Hydrogen project outlook

Powering the Grid: Hydrogen for Energy Storage and Electricity

Hydrogen enables long-duration and seasonal energy storage, converting excess renewable electricity into storable fuel. Utah’s ACES Delta project stores 150,000 MWh per cavern in underground salt formations.

Gas turbines at the Intermountain Power Project will transition to 100% hydrogen by 2045.

France’s HYFLEXPOWER project successfully demonstrated 100% green hydrogen power generation.

Conclusion: The Hydrogen Horizon

Hydrogen is no longer theoretical — it is being built now. From green steel and zero-emission transport to grid-scale energy storage, hydrogen is becoming a foundational pillar of the global net-zero economy.

It uniquely aligns climate progress, industrial competitiveness, and energy security.

Hydrogen allows us to fuel the world without burning the planet.
