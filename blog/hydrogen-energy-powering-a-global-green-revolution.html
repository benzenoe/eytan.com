Hydrogen Energy: Powering a Global Green Revolution

July 10, 2025 • Eytan Benzeno

Hydrogen Energy: Powering a Global Green Revolution

Figure: A fuel-cell vehicle (Toyota Mirai) refueling at a California hydrogen station, while an Air Products “SmartFuel” hydrogen delivery truck supplies the fuel. Hydrogen enables zero-emission transport with fast refueling similar to conventional fuels.

⸻

From Grey to Green – The Spectrum of Hydrogen Types

Hydrogen comes in many colors (figuratively) depending on how it’s produced. Traditionally most hydrogen has been grey, made from natural gas without capturing emissions, but newer low-carbon types are rising.

Green Hydrogen

Produced by splitting water (electrolysis) using renewable electricity (solar, wind, etc.), yielding hydrogen and oxygen with zero CO₂ emissions. Green hydrogen is currently a small share of supply, but like solar and wind power, its cost is expected to fall sharply with scale. https://www.iea.org/reports/global-hydrogen-review-2025

Blue Hydrogen

Made from fossil fuels (usually natural gas) via steam methane reforming, paired with carbon capture to trap the CO₂ byproduct. Blue hydrogen still generates CO₂ during production, but captures most of it for storage. https://www.iea.org/news/low-emissions-hydrogen-projects-grow-as-policy-support-races-to-catch-up

White Hydrogen

Naturally occurring hydrogen found in underground geological formations. It requires no industrial production and could cost under $1/kg. Only pilot projects exist today. https://www.iea.org/reports/global-hydrogen-review-2024/hydrogen-production

All hydrogen fuel is emission-free at point of use, producing only water vapor.

⸻

Safety Matters: Hydrogen’s Safety Profile

Hydrogen is non-toxic and disperses upward rapidly due to its low molecular weight. It is flammable and requires leak detection, ventilation, and proper materials to avoid embrittlement.

Industry has decades of experience handling hydrogen safely. Studies show hydrogen can be as safe as gasoline or natural gas when standards are followed. https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-hydrogen

⸻

Driving the Future: Hydrogen in Transport

Fuel cell electric vehicles (FCEVs) emit only water. Models include Toyota Mirai and Hyundai Nexo. Over 90,000 FCEVs were on the road globally by 2024.

Asia-Pacific leads adoption. South Korea targets 6.2 million hydrogen vehicles by 2040. https://www.iea.org/policies/6566-korea-hydrogen-economy-roadmap-2040

Hydrogen refuels in minutes and excels in heavy-duty transport. Airbus targets hydrogen-powered aviation by 2035.

⸻

Fueling Industry: Hydrogen’s Role in Industrial Decarbonization

Steel

HYBRIT produced the world’s first fossil-free steel using hydrogen, delivered to Volvo. https://www.hybritdevelopment.se/en/the-worlds-first-fossil-free-steel-ready-for-delivery/ https://www.reuters.com/business/sustainable-business/swedens-hybrit-delivers-worlds-first-fossil-free-steel-2021-08-18/

Chemicals and Fertilizers

Green hydrogen enables low-carbon ammonia. Companies like Yara and CF Industries are investing heavily.

The IEA identifies hydrogen as critical for hard-to-abate sectors. https://www.iea.org/news/low-emissions-hydrogen-projects-are-set-to-grow-strongly-despite-wave-of-cancellations-and-persistent-challenges

⸻

Powering the Grid: Hydrogen for Energy Storage and Electricity

Hydrogen enables long-duration energy storage. Utah’s ACES Delta project stores 150,000 MWh per cavern. https://www.energy.gov/lpo/advanced-clean-energy-storage https://www.wsp.com/en-us/projects/aces-delta-i-hydrogen-production-and-storage

Gas turbines will transition to 100% hydrogen by 2045. https://power.mhi.com/regions/amer/news/20230727.html

France demonstrated 100% hydrogen power generation (HYFLEXPOWER). https://cordis.europa.eu/article/id/447634-gas-turbine-runs-with-100-green-hydrogen-a-world-first

Investment Boom and Market Trends • $110+ billion committed to hydrogen projects https://hydrogencouncil.com/en/global-hydrogen-industry-surpasses-usd-110-billion-in-committed-investment-as-500-projects-worldwide-reach-maturity/ • 500+ projects at final investment decision https://compass.hydrogencouncil.com/ • China manufactures ~60% of global electrolyzer capacity https://www.iea.org/energy-system/low-emission-fuels/electrolysers

Global Momentum: Policies and Initiatives Worldwide

Europe

EU targets 20 million tonnes of renewable hydrogen by 2030. https://energy.ec.europa.eu/topics/eus-energy-system/hydrogen_en https://ehb.eu/files/downloads/European-Hydrogen-Backbone-April-2021-V3.pdf

North America

U.S. launched $8B Regional Hydrogen Hubs program. https://www.energy.gov/oced/regional-clean-hydrogen-hubs-0 https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/briefing-room/statements-releases/2023/10/13/

Asia-Pacific

Japan targets 12 Mt hydrogen use by 2040 with ¥15 trillion investment. https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/japan-aims-boost-hydrogen-supply-12-mln-t-by-2040-2023-04-04/

⸻

Conclusion: The Hydrogen Horizon

Hydrogen is being built now—fueling transport, industry, and power systems worldwide. It offers a rare alignment of climate progress, economic growth, and energy security.

Hydrogen allows us to fuel the world without burning the planet.
